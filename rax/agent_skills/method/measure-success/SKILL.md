# Measure the success condition and keep a margin

TASK.md states the task's success condition. It is the definition; your own margin against it is
something you can measure. Nothing on the real robot tells you the task is complete: you check the
condition yourself, from observations, and the operator confirms.

- **Record how close you ran.** What your perception said at the moment you judged the condition
  met, and in failed attempts how far you were from the condition and in which direction, shows
  how close you run.
- **Aim for the middle, not the edge.** A can released at the bin's wall bounces out; one released
  over the centre does not. A result that only just meets the condition succeeds in one scene and
  fails in the next; aiming well inside regions and well past thresholds holds up better.
- **What is not required.** If TASK.md does not ask you to let go, move on, stay still or keep
  holding, time spent on it is lost. If it does ask (an end pose, hands open, a distance from the
  table), doing it deliberately avoids surprises.
- **"Looks done" is not "is done".** A visual impression is not the condition; the measured quantity
  is (a height above the support, a position inside the target's extent, an angle). When the camera
  cannot see the result (the object fell behind the bin's rim), look again from another view or ask
  the operator before you report success.
- **Margins next to success counts** warn you of a shrinking margin before the count drops.
